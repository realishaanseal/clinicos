# ClinicOS Pilot Agreement

**Free 90-day pilot · Plain-language agreement including data processing terms**

> ⚠️ **This is a working draft, not legal advice.** It is written to be signable at a pilot clinic today and to cover the obligations that actually matter at this stage. Before you sign anything beyond free pilots — and certainly before you take money or a pharma partner — have a company secretary or lawyer review it. In West Bengal this typically costs a few thousand rupees and is worth it once real money is involved. Clause 8 in particular carries commercial weight well beyond the pilot.

### 1. What this is

We are giving the Clinic free access to ClinicOS for a 90-day pilot. ClinicOS records consultations with patient consent, and produces a draft clinical note and a printable prescription for the Doctor to review.

There is no fee during the pilot. There is no obligation on either side to continue afterwards.

### 2. The Doctor decides everything

ClinicOS produces **drafts only**. No note, prescription or record is final until the Doctor has reviewed and approved it.

ClinicOS does not diagnose, does not prescribe, and does not make clinical decisions. **All clinical judgement and all clinical responsibility remain entirely with the Doctor**, exactly as they were before ClinicOS was installed. Nothing in this agreement transfers any clinical responsibility to us.

The Doctor agrees not to sign or issue any output without reading it.

### 3. Patient consent

No consultation is recorded without the patient's consent, obtained before the consultation begins, using the consent script and form we provide.

The Clinic is responsible for obtaining that consent. We are responsible for providing the script, the form and a product that cannot record without consent being recorded first.

If a patient declines, the Doctor proceeds on paper as normal and the patient's care is unaffected in any way.

### 4. Who is responsible for the data

The Clinic is the **Data Fiduciary** for its patients' personal data. We act as a **Data Processor** on the Clinic's instructions.

This means we will:

- process patient data **only** to provide ClinicOS to this Clinic, and for no other purpose;
- apply reasonable technical and organisational security measures to protect it;
- tell the Clinic in writing about any third-party service we use to process the data (our current list is at Schedule A), and not add new ones without notice;
- keep the Clinic's records only where the Clinic chose: on its own computer, on ClinicOS online (stored in India), or both kept in step;
- delete the **raw audio recording** of each consultation once the Doctor has approved and signed the resulting note, wherever it was recorded;
- put a signed prescription online for a patient only when the Doctor shares it with that patient's separate consent, and take it down when the Doctor or the patient asks;
- not transfer patient data to anyone else except as set out in this agreement or as required by law.

### 5. No pharmaceutical access. Ever.

We will never give any pharmaceutical company, medical representative, distributor or similar party:

- any patient's identity or data, in any form;
- any information about what this Doctor prescribed, to whom, or how often;
- any ability to influence, appear within, or receive data about any individual prescribing decision.

This restriction survives the end of this agreement and cannot be waived by us for any commercial reason.

### 6. If something goes wrong

If we become aware of any unauthorised access to or loss of patient data, we will notify the Clinic **without delay and in any event within 24 hours**, and will cooperate fully with the Clinic in meeting its own notification obligations to the Data Protection Board of India and, where applicable, CERT-In.

### 7. The Clinic's records belong to the Clinic

All patient records created through ClinicOS belong to the Clinic.

At any time, and on termination for any reason, we will provide the Clinic with a complete export of its records in a usable format (PDF and a structured data file), free of charge, within 7 days of request.

We will not hold records hostage, degrade access, or charge for export under any circumstances.

### 8. Improving the system

To improve ClinicOS — particularly its ability to understand Bengali, Hindi and mixed-language speech — we may use **de-identified** consultation text: patient name, contact details, address, and any other direct identifier permanently removed.

The Clinic may opt out of this by ticking here: ☐ *(The Clinic keeps full access to ClinicOS either way.)*

We will never use identifiable patient data for this purpose, and we will never use it to build anything for a pharmaceutical company.

### 9. Ending the pilot

Either side may end this agreement at any time, for any reason or none, with 7 days' written notice (a WhatsApp message is fine).

On termination we export the Clinic's records under clause 7 and delete our copies within 30 days, except where we are required by law to retain them.

### 10. Support and honesty

We will respond to support requests within one working day during the pilot.

In exchange, we ask the Clinic to tell us honestly when ClinicOS is not working or not being used — including if the Doctor simply stops using it. **Knowing that it failed is more valuable to us than being told it worked.**

## How to use this document

- **Keep it to two pages when printed.** A doctor will read two pages. They will not read eight, and a long agreement reads as something to be suspicious of.
- **Walk through it verbally in five minutes**, in Bengali. Do not hand it over silently. The clauses that will earn you trust — clause 2 (you decide everything), clause 5 (no pharma, ever), clause 7 (your records, exportable, free) — are worth saying out loud.
- **Sign two copies.** Leave one at the clinic. Doctors keep paper.
- **Clause 8 is the one to be scrupulous about.** It is the legal basis for the language data flywheel your deck calls a moat. If you take that data without a clear clause and a real opt-out, the moat is built on something you cannot defend. Be straight about it and most doctors will not object.
