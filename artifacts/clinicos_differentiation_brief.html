Claims no competitor is making

Verified against each company's own site and coverage. Safe to state on a deck without hedging.

Raw consultation audio is deleted the moment the doctor signs the note
Eka Care and HealthPlix are both retained-data, cloud-EMR platforms — that's structurally what an EMR is. Nothing in either company's public materials commits to deleting audio. For a doctor or patient wary of a national platform holding recordings, this is a trust pitch neither competitor can easily match without rebuilding their own architecture.
No retention-deletion policy found on eka.care or healthplix.com
Built for solo, non-tech-native doctors in small towns — not hospital IT departments
Eka Care is architected around ABDM, FHIR, AWS-scale infrastructure: 33,000+ facilities, 90,000+ doctors, hospital-grade compliance. That scale shapes its onboarding and support model toward institutions, not a single practitioner with no IT staff. Hands-on, same-day setup for someone like a Krishnanagar OPD doctor isn't what that architecture optimizes for.
Eka Care public materials emphasize NHA/FHIR/HIPAA/AWS compliance, not solo-clinic onboarding
One connected loop — scribe, timeline, monitoring, referral — for small clinics specifically
Eka Care covers scribe + EMR. HealthPlix covers EMR + prescriptions. Neither connects through to chronic monitoring and referral routing as one product built for a solo clinic's actual patient journey, rather than a hospital's departmental workflow.
No single competitor product spans all four stages for the solo/small-clinic segment

What would make this more unique

Ranked by how much they change the competitive picture, not by build effort.

Closing fast, not optional

ABDM / ABHA integration

Over 73 crore ABHA IDs and 3.6 lakh clinics are already registered in India's Health Facility Registry. Eka Care already has this; ClinicOS's roadmap doesn't. Non-compliance risks exclusion from national schemes, referrals, and insurance tie-ups — this is heading toward a requirement, not a nice-to-have differentiator.

Testable now

Measured dialect accuracy

Once pilot data exists, benchmark transcription accuracy on the specific colloquial Bengali/Hindi code-switching your target doctors use, against Eka Care's broader 15-language model. "More accurate on your patients' actual speech" is a defensible number; "supports Bengali" is not.

White space, high regulatory care

Commercial chronic monitoring for small clinics

No competitor sells this to small clinics today — SIMPLE is a government program run by frontline workers, not a product. Real opportunity, but this is also the exact point the roadmap crosses into CDSCO SaMD territory, so it needs a deliberate regulatory decision before scaling, not a default build.

Compounds an existing edge

Make the deletion policy visible to patients

Right now it's a backend decision. Surfacing it at the point of consultation — something the patient sees and understands, not just a privacy-policy line — turns an engineering choice into a trust feature competitors would have to rebuild their architecture to copy.

Depends on Week 4 gate

Patient-facing positioning, not just time-saved

If the documentation-burden pitch underperforms, the pivot hypothesis — branded prescriptions, recall messaging, clinic prestige — is itself a wedge: both Eka Care and HealthPlix market as scribe/EMR-first, not patient-experience-first.
