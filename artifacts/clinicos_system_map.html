Two pipelines running on the same clock. The product lane is what a doctor's consultation turns into, step by step. The validation lane is the evidence program that decides, at three fixed checkpoints, whether to keep building it. They meet at two points: pilot data feeds the real cost model, and the monitoring phase is the point the product crosses into regulated territory.

Known weak points carried into this map

Regulatory line is function-specific

The "no regulatory approval needed" claim is true for phases 1–2 (scribe, timeline) and false once chronic monitoring quantifies physiological parameters and fires risk alerts. That's the flag on the diagram above.

Unit economics are tighter than modeled

Placeholder inference costs put gross-margin breakeven near ₹2,500/clinic/month, above the deck's ₹2,000 assumption. Real cost-per-consultation must come from week 4 pilot data, not the placeholder.

10x claim vs. 35–45% documentation figure

The deck's own documentation-burden number implies at most ~1.8x, not 10x. The 35–45% figure is largely drawn from Western EHR studies where the burden is billing/coding compliance — a category cash-pay Indian OPD mostly lacks.

Referral moat and monitoring assumption

The network-effect moat is asserted, not demonstrated — the first ~200 clinics get zero network value. Slide 8's 500-monitored-patients-per-clinic figure is the softest number in the model. Eka Care's Feb 2026 offline multilingual scribe (with NVIDIA) undercuts the competition slide.

Why the lanes are linked, not separate

This isn't two projects running side by side. The pilot tracker's real cost-per-consultation replaces the placeholder economics live; the moment the product build reaches chronic monitoring, that's the same moment the incorporation trigger and the SaMD regulatory path both activate.
